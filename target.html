How to read this page

MOVE is organised in houses, each led by a head who reports to the COO. Every seat sits on one of four layers that say how far its work reaches: the whole company, every country from HQ, one country, or one branch. A seat reports up its own house; the Country Manager directs people on the ground in their country on local matters.

Today shows where every seat sits now. Target shows the structure we are moving to over the next year. Click any seat to see what it does, who it reports to, the number it owns, and what changes. The How it works tab explains the hand-offs between houses, one owner per step. The Measures tab lists every seat's number and the split where two seats touch the same outcome.

Country specialists in every house report up their own house; the Country Manager of their country directs them on local matters. Edu-trainers are based in branches and take branch matters from the Branch Manager. Branch Pioneer is a status a Branch Manager carries, not a seat.

The hand-offs that make the structure work. Each chain has one owner per step, so a hand-over is never a gap. Every seat's card links to the chains it takes part in.

Every seat owns its numbers, and no number has two owners. For each seat, its own outcomes come first (what it holds whatever anyone else does), then what it owes another seat by a date. Where two seats touch the same outcome, the split below says which part each one owns, so the two numbers pull in the same direction instead of against each other.
